About the Lab

Selection, physiology,
and the cost of change

The Brown Greenhouse Lab investigates the physiological underpinnings of both natural and artificial selection in plants. While selection optimizes ecological and morphological traits, it inevitably drives corresponding physiological changes — and those changes may come with trade-offs in stress tolerance, water dynamics, lifespan, and resource use.

We seek to unravel these relationships. Our work spans crop science, comparative plant physiology, and ecophysiology, with a shared commitment to rigorous experimental design and meaningful questions.

Based at the University of Toronto Scarborough, we work in the greenhouse and field to understand how plants really work — and what selection asks of them.

Research

What we study

Our research program centres on understanding the physiological underpinnings of both natural and artificial selection. While both types of selection aim to optimize ecological and/or morphological characteristics of plants, corresponding physiological changes will likely occur in order to support these characteristics — but this could also lead to trade-offs in terms of, for example, stress tolerance or lifespan. We seek to unravel these relationships through carefully designed greenhouse experiments and comparative physiological analyses.

Brassica oleracea · Wildfire Smoke

Secondary Effects of Wildfire Smoke on Nutrient Content

How do the secondary effects of wildfire smoke affect the nutrient content of different cultivars of Brassica oleracea? This project investigates an increasingly relevant question as wildfire frequency rises globally.

Perennials · Oxidative Stress

Oxidative Stress and Interspecific Competition

How does exogenous oxidative stress mitigate competition between evergreen and deciduous perennials? This project explores stress physiology as a mediator of plant community dynamics.

Flax · Drought Tolerance

Life History and Drought Tolerance in Flax

How do life history, oil vs. fibre selection, and flowering time impact water dynamics and drought tolerance in flax? Selection history may carry hidden physiological costs.

Flax · Nutrient Dynamics

Selection History and Fertilizer Requirements in Flax

Do the same factors — life history, selection type, and flowering time — influence fertilizer requirements in flax? This project examines nutrient physiology through the lens of breeding history.

Brassica oleracea · Salt Tolerance

Cultivar Variation in Salt Tolerance

Is there variation in salt tolerance among different cultivars of Brassica oleracea? Understanding this variation has implications for both crop resilience and selection-driven physiology.
